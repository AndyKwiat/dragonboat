# Trading: cities, story events, VIPs, upkeep, time

Files: `src/trading/*` (rules, no Godot types — `Supply.cs` and
`Production.cs` are the economy proper and are unit-tested),
`src/core/GameData.cs` (cities/products), `src/core/Product.cs`,
`src/core/City.cs`, `src/core/GameTime.cs` (the clock),
`src/ui/CityPopup.cs` (the market panel), `src/ui/Clock.cs`
(purse/cargo/time readout), the
`// Market`, `// Story events`, `// Player`, `// Game time`,
`// Upkeep`, `// City popup` blocks of `src/core/Config.cs`. This repo is
the source of truth for these rules (it began as a port of a three.js
prototype, since deleted). Keep the `src/trading/` files free of Godot
types.
Combat never touches any of this; the only shared objects are the `Boat` and
the clock. The hold and the crew are the boat's own, out of `boats.yml`
([harbour.md](harbour.md)) — buying a boat in a harbour is the only thing
that changes either. Banking
(`src/banking/`, [banking.md](banking.md)) is its own system too — it only
moves the player's money and honour — but its bank row and button sit in
the city popup, and medium/large cities are the ones that have a bank.

## Data

`data/products.json` is canonical here (originally generated by the deleted
prototype's scripts); `GameData.Load()` reads it once. Every product carries
a `class`: **staple** (rice, fish, fruit, wood, wool) or **luxury** (clothes,
saffron, furniture, jewelry). The class decides who makes it, and nothing
else — a luxury is not inherently dearer than a staple, its base price is.

**Cities are generated fresh at every launch** — there is no cities.json.
`game-config.yml`'s `smallCities` / `mediumCities` / `largeCities` say how
many of each (24 / 12 / 4); `src/world/CityPlacer.cs` (Godot-free,
unit-tested in `tests/`) places them:

- **Sizes**: exactly what the config asks for — there is no ratio in the
  code. `CityPlacer.SizesFor` just lists them, large first (the 140-cell
  footprint is the hardest to fit, so it gets the pick of the coast).
  Story-event and VIP counts scale with the total (`GameConfig.CityCount`),
  and a world with no large city has nowhere for the boat to start or for
  monks to be carried to, so keep at least one.
- **Where**: on the coast. Every grass cell that touches water is collected
  and shuffled; each city takes the first one where its footprint fits —
  pushed inland from the coast cell so its edge is the shoreline, at least
  90% land (small bays get filled), fully inside the map, `MinSpacing`
  (300 cells) from every other city, `MapEdgeMargin` (200 cells) clear of
  the map's border — off-map counts as water, so the border would otherwise
  read as a fake coast — and still leaving water against its outside edge,
  so a city can never swallow the creek it sits on. The land
  requirement relaxes (90% → 70% → 50%) before the placer gives up; a map
  with no coast is a hard error.
- **Footprints** are stamped into the loaded map as city cells (red):
  60×60 small, 100×100 medium, 140×140 large (cells are ~1 m).
- **Names** are drawn without repeats from `data/city-names.json` (100 names).
- The boat starts on open water off a random large city's shore
  (`CityPlacer.WaterStartNear` — the nearest spot with
  `Config.BoatStartClearanceCells` (30) of clear water around it), pointing
  out to sea. The chosen city is printed at startup.

What a city produces and stocks, and every number behind its prices, is
[the economy](economy.md) — dealt fresh each run from `GameData.SetCities`.
In short: small cities make one staple and stock rice plus that staple,
medium ones make one staple and one luxury, large ones two luxuries, and
medium and large stock everything. A world that cannot support an economy is
a startup error, not a playable run.

## Rules

- **Markets and prices** are [their own system](economy.md): supply-driven
  prices, the per-unit ladder a trade walks, the daily decay, the world
  average every percentage is quoted against, and the Give button's
  threshold. Nothing in this file sets a price.
- **Story events** (`StoryEvents.cs`): the world always holds
  `game-config.yml`'s `priceEvents` supply shocks plus `vipEvents` VIP
  delivery offers (both required keys; 0 means that kind never spawns),
  each tied to a city — a city can hold several (but at most one VIP
  offer).
  `ProductPriceStoryEvent`: one product the city stocks — picked with weight
  1/(cities stocking it), so rare goods headline more events but ubiquitous
  rice keeps a small chance — never two on the same (city, product).
  **Rolling the event is the shock**: the city's supply for that product
  jumps by `StoryEventShockFraction` (0.8) of its market size there and then,
  a shortage (dear) `StoryEventShortageOddsToOne` (3) times as often as a
  glut (cheap). The daily decay walks it back from that moment, so the swing
  fades instead of switching off; the event object itself only carries the
  story and the expiry. Each expires at a midnight `StoryEventMinDays`–
  `StoryEventMaxDays` (5–10) days out; expiries only happen at midnight, so
  `StoryEvents.Update()` (run every frame from `Main`) is a single compare
  until the clock crosses one, then a single pass drops lapsed events and
  rolls replacements at random cities. When the pass runs — or when the
  midnight decay does — `Main` rebuilds the open city popup, whose sliders
  anchor on the supply level the row opened at, and the map labels' intel.
- **VIP deliveries** (`VipDeliveryStoryEvent`): a city hosts a passenger
  bound for another city ranked `VipMinRank`..`VipMaxRank` (6th..10th) by
  distance — never one of the 5 closest, never beyond the 10th. The offer
  expires like any event (a fresh one rolls elsewhere); its city's popup
  shows it at the top with an Accept button. Accepting retires the offer
  (`StoryEvents.Accept` immediately rolls a replacement, holding the count
  steady) and puts a `VipPassenger` — the destination plus the day they
  came aboard — in `Player.VipPassengers`. Passengers take no cargo space
  and stack (several may ride at once); the clock overlay shows one line
  each with what landing them right now is worth and how long is left
  before they give up, and the destination's map label carries a
  `▲ VIP TO LAND HERE` tag. Opening the destination's popup is what delivers
  (`Player.DeliverVips`, summarised in a note at the top of that popup):
  `VipDeliveryMoney` (200) and `VipDeliveryHonour` (10) per head, plus the
  speed bonus below.
- **The VIP clock** (`Vips.cs`, terms in `Config.Vips`): the passage is
  timed from the day the passenger came aboard.
  `Vips.SpeedHonourFor(daysAboard)` pays `SpeedHonour` (200) for landing
  them inside the first day and `SpeedHonourPerDay` (20) less for every
  further day, so day 2 is worth 180, day 6 is 100, and from day 11 the
  bonus is gone and only the flat 10 honour remains. Keep one aboard for
  `AbandonDays` (15) days and they give up: the passenger leaves the ship,
  `AbandonHonour` (1000) honour goes with them (honour may go negative),
  and the event log says so — with a warning each of the `WarnDays` (3)
  days before it happens. `Vips.Update` runs on the same daily beat as the
  bank loans (`Main`'s `DayTurn`; `Days.Of` turns the clock's minutes into
  the whole-day index both use — `src/core/News.cs`).
- **Rumours**: a city's popup opens with the `RumourCount` (3)
  geographically nearest events that are NOT the city's own
  (`StoryEvents.RumoursFor`), one line each from the event's
  `Description` ("Fish prices are very high in Sword Hill"). Seeing a
  rumour marks that event discovered, so neighbouring markets spread the
  news — the player needn't visit every city.
- **Docking** (`Docking.cs`, wired in `Main._Process`): running the hull
  into a city opens its market by itself — no click needed. The boat counts
  as at the quay when it comes within `CityDockContactMargin` (8 units) of
  the city's square footprint (`CityPlacer.Touching`, nearest city wins);
  the hull's three collision circles put its nose against land at about 7,
  so this fires on contact and not while sailing past. Two rules stop the
  market snapping straight back open when you close it: you have to **leave**
  — the city is remembered while the hull is still against it, whether the
  market was opened by the collision or by clicking the label — and a
  `CityDockCooldownSec` (5 real seconds) cooldown runs from every close, so
  bumping back in, or drifting into the quay next door, does nothing until
  it lapses. The market never opens over an already-open one.
- **Discovery**: opening a city's popup marks the city discovered forever
  (`City.Discovered`) and marks every event live there discovered
  (`StoryEvent.Discovered`; via `StoryEvents.DiscoverAt`, also called when
  the popup rebuilds at midnight while open). Rumours discover events
  remotely, so an event can be discovered while its city is not. On the map,
  under a city's name label (`CityLabels.RefreshIntel`): a discovered shock
  shows its product, price and days left even if the city itself is
  undiscovered; a discovered city additionally shows its most off-base
  prices, capped and thresholded (see UI below). An undiscovered shock on a
  discovered city shows nothing for that product — the player hasn't seen
  that market since it began.
- **Player** (`Player.cs`): purse (never below 0), honour (starts 0, may
  go negative; shown beside money in the clock overlay and the popup's
  purse line), the boat she sails (`Player.Boat`, which is where
  `CrewCount` and `CargoCapacity()` come from), inventory. Honour is also what a city's bank
  lends against, what repaying one in time earns, and what a late loan
  costs ([banking.md](banking.md)).
  `Buy` clamps to hold space AND the purse, `Sell`/`Consume` to what's
  held. It also carries the hold's book value and implements `ITrader`, which is all a market deal
  needs of it — the trading arithmetic itself is
  [the economy's](economy.md).
- **Crew**: not hired and not a choice — the head count is the boat's, and
  so is the hold ([harbour.md](harbour.md)). A bigger boat therefore eats
  more rice and costs more in wages, which is what makes the large one worth
  thinking about rather than simply better.
- **Upkeep** (`Upkeep.cs`): `Due` counts periods of game time elapsed since
  the last check (catching up over big jumps). `Rations`: one unit of
  `RationsProductId` (rice) per crew member per `RationsHoursPerUnit`;
  `Wages`: `WagesMoneyPerDay` per head per day, stopping at an empty purse
  (the crew goes unpaid, money never goes negative). Both run in `Main`
  every frame and tell the open popup to refresh when something was
  charged.
- **Game time** (`GameTime.cs`): 30-day months, 24 h clock, only moves when
  the player acts: sailing advances `HoursPerSecondSailing` per (fast-forwarded)
  second under way (trading itself costs no time). Holding either Shift
  fast-forwards the voyage (`TimeScaleForFastForward`): the rate ramps
  from 1× to `FastForwardMaxRate` (5×) over `FastForwardRampUpSec` (1.5)
  of holding, and drains back over `FastForwardRampDownSec` (1) on
  release so the return to normal isn't jarring. The boat covers more
  ground and the clock moves by the same factor. Night is
  `NightStartHour`..`NightEndHour` (the clock tints).

## UI

Click a city's name (`ui/CityLabels.cs` does the hit-testing) to open
`CityPopup`: facts (including what the city **makes** and its market size),
rumours, the harbour (medium and large cities — the boats for sale,
[harbour.md](harbour.md)), the bank row (medium and large
cities — its button opens the bank window, [banking.md](banking.md)), then
one row per stocked product with a trade slider. What the slider does, what
each row shows and every price in it belong to
[the economy](economy.md#how-it-reads-on-screen); the popup only draws them.

Esc, the ✕, or a click outside closes it. See [ui.md](ui.md) for
sizing/fonts. While the popup is open the world is paused
for input: no sailing, no camera pan/zoom, no cannon fire — and the click
that closes it is inert (see `menuOpen` in `Main._Process`).
